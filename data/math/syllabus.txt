MATHEMATICS — SYLLABUS
Grade 10 · Academic Track · Latest revision

This document defines the units, learning objectives, and assessment focus for
the Mathematics course. Questions in tests and examinations are drawn from the
topics listed below. Students should be able to explain every bold concept in
their own words and to solve at least one problem from each formula family.

Unit 1 — Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Learning objectives: simplify and factorise polynomial expressions; solve linear
equations and inequalities; manipulate algebraic fractions; solve simultaneous
linear equations in two unknowns (substitution and elimination methods).
Key terms: coefficient, constant, like terms, identity, root, solution set.
Example: Simplify 3(x - 2) + 4x(2x + 1) = 3x - 6 + 8x^2 + 4x = 8x^2 + 7x - 6.
Assessment focus: expanding brackets, collecting like terms, factorising by
common factor and by grouping, solving ax + b = c, and systems such as
2x + y = 7 and x - 3y = -4.

Unit 2 — Functions and Graphs
Learning objectives: interpret function notation f(x); find domains and ranges;
plot linear and quadratic functions; identify gradient and y-intercept from
y = mx + c; understand the effect of c and m on a line.
Key terms: domain, range, gradient, intercept, axis of symmetry, vertex.
Example: For f(x) = 2x - 3, the gradient is 2 and the y-intercept is -3, so
the graph rises 2 units for every 1 unit moved right.
Assessment focus: reading values off graphs, sketching straight lines, and
stating maximum/minimum points of simple quadratics.

Unit 3 — Quadratic Equations
Learning objectives: solve quadratic equations by factorisation, by completing
the square, and with the quadratic formula x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a);
interpret the discriminant b^2 - 4ac; link roots to the graph crossing the axis.
Key terms: discriminant, roots, solutions, perfect square.
Example: Solve x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 by factorising to (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0, so the
roots are x = 2 and x = 3.
Assessment focus: applying the correct method, stating roots accurately, and
explaining what a negative discriminant means for the graph.

Unit 4 — Coordinate Geometry
Learning objectives: use the distance formula, midpoint formula, and gradient
formula between two points; find the equation of a line given two points or a
point and gradient; recognise parallel and perpendicular lines by gradient.
Key terms: midpoint, distance, perpendicular, parallel, equation of a line.
Example: The midpoint of (1, 2) and (5, 6) is ((1+5)/2, (2+6)/2) = (3, 4).
Assessment focus: manipulating coordinates without a calculator and justifying
why two lines are parallel (equal gradients) or perpendicular (product is -1).

Unit 5 — Sequences and Series
Learning objectives: find the nth term of arithmetic and geometric sequences;
compute partial sums of arithmetic series; recognise when a geometric series
diverges or converges.
Key terms: common difference, common ratio, term, sum, convergence.
Example: 3, 7, 11, 15, ... is arithmetic with common difference 4 and nth term
a_n = 3 + (n - 1) * 4 = 4n - 1.
Assessment focus: writing the general term, using the sum formula
S_n = (n/2)(a_1 + a_n), and explaining convergence tests in words.

Unit 6 — Introduction to Calculus
Learning objectives: understand limits informally; compute the derivative of
polynomials using the power rule; interpret the derivative as the gradient of a
tangent and as an instantaneous rate of change.
Key terms: limit, derivative, tangent, rate of change, power rule.
Example: If y = x^3 + 2x, then dy/dx = 3x^2 + 2, which gives the slope of the
curve at any x.
Assessment focus: differentiating simple polynomials, evaluating f'(a) at a
point, and describing what the derivative tells us about a motion problem.
