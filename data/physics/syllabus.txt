PHYSICS — SYLLABUS
Grade 10 · Academic Track · Latest revision

This document defines the units, learning objectives, and assessment focus for
the Physics course. Laboratory work is assessed against these units, and written
examinations always state the unit from which each question is drawn.

Unit 1 — Measurements and Units
Learning objectives: use the SI base units (metre, kilogram, second, ampere,
kelvin, mole, candela); convert between prefixes (milli, centi, kilo, mega);
estimate uncertainty and round to significant figures consistently.
Key terms: precision, accuracy, uncertainty, significant figures, prefix.
Example: 2.5 km = 2.5 x 10^3 m = 2500 m; a reading of 12.34 cm is good to four
significant figures.
Assessment focus: converting units, quoting answers to the correct number of
significant figures, and choosing the right instrument for a measurement.

Unit 2 — Kinematics
Learning objectives: define displacement, velocity, acceleration; interpret
position-time and velocity-time graphs; apply uniformly accelerated motion
equations v = u + at, s = ut + 0.5at^2, and v^2 = u^2 + 2as.
Key terms: displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, uniform motion.
Example: A car accelerates from rest at 2 m/s^2 for 5 s, so v = 0 + 2(5) = 10
m/s and s = 0 + 0.5(2)(25) = 25 m.
Assessment focus: reading gradients and areas off graphs, choosing the correct
kinematics equation, and handling direction signs.

Unit 3 — Laws of Motion
Learning objectives: state Newton's three laws; apply F = ma to single objects
and simple systems; understand inertia, mass versus weight, and action-reaction
pairs; solve problems with friction as a constant resistive force.
Key terms: inertia, mass, weight, net force, friction, reaction pair.
Example: Weight = mass x gravitational field strength, W = mg, so a 50 kg
student on Earth has weight W = 50 x 9.8 = 490 N.
Assessment focus: drawing force diagrams, summing forces before applying
F = ma, and stating which law explains a given everyday situation.

Unit 4 — Work, Energy and Power
Learning objectives: define work W = Fd cos(theta); distinguish kinetic energy
KE = 0.5mv^2 and gravitational potential energy PE = mgh; state the work-energy
principle; compute power P = W/t.
Key terms: work, energy, power, kinetic, potential, conservation.
Example: Lifting a 10 kg box 2 m gives it PE = 10 x 9.8 x 2 = 196 J of stored
gravitational energy.
Assessment focus: energy conservation in ramps and swings, work done by constant
forces, and power values for everyday appliances.

Unit 5 — Waves and Sound
Learning objectives: describe transverse and longitudinal waves; relate
wave speed v = f(lambda) to frequency and wavelength; describe reflection,
refraction, and diffraction qualitatively; discuss hearing range and loudness.
Key terms: amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period, medium, compression.
Example: If a wave travels at 340 m/s with a frequency of 680 Hz, its
wavelength is lambda = v/f = 340/680 = 0.5 m.
Assessment focus: labelling wave diagrams, applying v = f(lambda), and explaining
why sound needs a medium but light does not.

Unit 6 — Electricity and Circuits
Learning objectives: use Ohm's law V = IR; interpret series and parallel
circuits; compute equivalent resistance; apply power P = VI and energy
E = Pt; describe current, voltage, and resistance in words.
Key terms: current, voltage, resistance, series, parallel, short circuit.
Example: A 12 V battery drives 2 A through a lamp, so the lamp resistance is
R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 ohms and its power is 24 W.
Assessment focus: solving two-loop-ish series/parallel problems, safe-unit
conversions, and predicting brightness changes when components are added.
